The battery rebate, explained - October 2026

How the federal battery discount works, and why January matters

The Cheaper Home Batteries Program has been taking thousands off home batteries since July 2025. It was reshaped on 1 May 2026 and now steps down every six months. Here is what that means if you are thinking about a battery.

8.4STCs/kWh

5.2STCs/kWh
Keeps stepping down every six months until the program ends in 2030.

It comes off the price, not back to you later. The discount is created as small-scale technology certificates (STCs) for each kWh of usable battery capacity. Your installer claims them and takes the value off your quote upfront - a good quote shows it as its own line.

Bigger batteries get a tapering rate. Since 1 May 2026 the first 14 kWh earns the full rate, capacity between 14 and 28 kWh earns 60%, and 28 to 50 kWh earns 15%. Nothing above 50 kWh counts. That is why so many home quotes now sit at or just under 14 kWh.

Your installation date sets the rate. Not the date you get a quote, sign a contract or pay a deposit. A battery quoted in November but installed on 5 January gets the January rate.

The practical upshot for October 2026: with about -- days left, the homeowners who keep the higher rate are the ones who get a quote now, give themselves time to compare it properly, and lock an installation date before the Christmas shutdown. Ask any installer to put the installation date - and the discount at that date - in writing.

What makes a battery eligible. The home must be connected to the grid, the battery must be on the approved product list and capable of joining a virtual power plant (you do not have to join one), and it must be installed by an accredited installer. Every installer we match you with meets that bar.

Straight talk

Is a battery actually worth it for your home?

A deadline is a reason to get a quote, not a reason to buy the wrong battery. Here is who tends to come out ahead, and who should think harder.

A battery usually stacks up if...

  • You export a lot of solar in the middle of the day for a few cents per kWh.
  • Most of your power is used in the evening - cooking, heating or cooling, kids home after school.
  • You are on (or can move to) a time-of-use plan with an expensive evening peak.
  • You want backup through outages, an EV charged on your own sun, or to go all-electric.

Think harder if...

  • Your solar system is small and barely covers daytime use - there is little left to store.
  • Someone is home all day and already uses most of the solar as it is made.
  • You are planning to sell within a couple of years.
  • A quote is pushing a big battery without showing your usage - bigger is not automatically better under the tiered rate.

Batteries in Bendigo

What's different about a battery in Bendigo

Victoria's state battery rebate under Solar Homes has closed, so the federal discount is the main incentive for Bendigo homes - which makes its 1 January step-down matter more here than in states that still stack a second rebate.

Feed-in tariffs are close to nothing. Victoria's minimum feed-in tariff has been cut to a few cents or less, so the gap between exporting a kilowatt-hour and buying one back in the evening is wide.
Size for winter, not just summer. Victorian winters cut solar output hard. A battery sized to fill on a January afternoon may never fill in June, so a good quote shows both seasons.
Going all-electric. Households swapping gas for heat pumps and induction lift their evening use - which is when a battery pays its way.
Network rules. Your network is Powercor. Export limits are set by the network, so ask what applies at your address before agreeing on a size.
